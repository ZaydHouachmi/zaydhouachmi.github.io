ZEIKU: a blood-sample swab drying system

Ottawa Police labs dry forensic swabs, such as blood samples taken for DNA analysis, by hand and judge dryness by eye. A badly dried swab can spoil the sample, skew results and risk cross-contamination. I led a five-person team through the design, fabrication, assembly and testing of a sealed, portable box that dries two swabs side by side, measures humidity in real time and lights up when each swab is dry.

What I did

  • Led the team through design, fabrication, assembly and testing, and delivered a working prototype that met the client's main objective.
  • Created SolidWorks parts and assemblies and fabricated the components by 3D printing and laser cutting.
  • Integrated the Arduino temperature and humidity sensors, then analyzed the drying-test data and reported the findings.

What the client needed

  • Drying without handling during the process, and no opening the box to check.
  • Clear, reliable confirmation that a swab is dry.
  • A compact, hygienic device that is easy to clean.
  • Automatic temperature and humidity logging to prove the process worked.

Mechanical design

  • Enclosure: rigid, non-porous acrylic that is easy to disinfect, reinforced with 3D-printed PLA brackets in the corners and along the edges.
  • Sliding door on rails, so the box opens in one motion without disturbing the samples.
  • Rail-mounted, removable shelves for the swab holder and the electronics. Everything comes out for cleaning without tools.
  • Ventilation: 5×5 cm cut-out, 3D-printed fan mount with M3 screws, outer grille and a foam-filter frame, so only filtered air reaches the chamber.

Iteration

From prototype 1 to final

Prototype 1 was tested in November 2025 and reviewed with the client on November 3. Each failure turned into a design change in the final version.

Where it came fromPrototype 1Final version
Door testFailed. The removable door hit the top of the box and cracked it on every attempt to close it.A sliding door that runs in two rails, with the fit and tolerances reworked.
Detection testFailed. The LED stayed off after the swab had dried, until someone stirred the air by hand.A 5 V, 40 mm fan keeps filtered air moving past the sensors.
Client reviewFan mounted on the back wall. The client asked for it in the centre.Fan moved to the centre of the box for more even airflow.
CapacityOne swab sensor and one LED.Three sensors (ambient plus one per swab) and two LEDs, for two swabs side by side.
Load test3 mm panels flexed visibly from 7.8 N.PLA brackets added in the corners and along the edges.
Client reviewApproved. Acrylic wipes clean, and the compact size travels well between sites.Kept.

Load-testing the panels

The 3 mm acrylic panels take the handle load and the knocks of transport. We loaded them in ten steps with water, from 100 mL to 1000 mL (0.98 N to 9.81 N), and watched for deflection.

6.9N

No permanent deformation at any step, so the acrylic stayed elastic. For a handle that carries the whole box, the recommendation was internal ribs or 4–5 mm sheet.
